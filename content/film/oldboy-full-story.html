Fifteen years in a room with no window, no explanation, and a television. Then one day the door opens. Oldboy is the film that made the world take Korean cinema seriously, and two decades later its ending still lands like a car crash. Here is the whole story — the imprisonment, the hammer, the octopus, and the reveal that people still refuse to spoil for their friends.

The setup

Seoul, 1988. Oh Dae-su (Choi Min-sik) is drunk on his daughter's fourth birthday when he is picked up by police for causing a scene. His friend Joo-hwan bails him out, and then Dae-su disappears — grabbed off the street, drugged, and locked in a room that looks like a cheap hotel, with a television, a bed, and a door that will not open.

He is kept there for fifteen years. He learns from the news on that television that his wife has been murdered — and that he is the prime suspect. He spends the years training his body against the wall, and writing a list of everyone he has ever wronged, looking for a reason.

The story, start to end

1. Released

One night he is gassed, and wakes up on a rooftop with a phone, a wallet, and a tailor who arrives to fit him for a suit. Dae-su is free, and his only purpose is to find whoever did this and kill him. A young woman, Mi-do (Kang Hye-jung), finds him collapsed and, for reasons he cannot understand, takes him in.

2. The hammer

He starts hunting. A dumpling restaurant leads him to the man who held him — and to the most famous single-take fight in modern cinema: two minutes of Dae-su, hammer in hand, in a corridor against twenty men, filmed sideways like a video game, exhausting rather than cool. He beats them, gets stabbed, and crawls out.

The corridor fight, one continuous take. Photo: Show East

3. The villain introduces himself

The man who imprisoned him, Lee Woo-jin (Yoo Ji-tae), a wealthy, beautiful, patient man in a pale suit, does not hide. He phones Dae-su. He says the game is not finished: find out why you were locked up in five days, and I will end my life. Fail, and I will end yours. Dae-su finds himself in a private penthouse, sedated with Mi-do beside him — and the two of them sleep together.

He discovers his own imprisonment was financed by the same person, that the corridor guard recorded everything, and that his daughter's old school photograph has been altered to look like Mi-do. Meanwhile, Lee Woo-jin's own memory is tied to a high-school corridor: he and his sister were in love, Dae-su saw them together and gossiped about it, and the story — a rumour he does not even remember starting — destroyed their family. His sister killed herself.

4. The twist (last chance to look away)

The trap closes on the 14th floor of the penthouse. Lee Woo-jin reveals the price of the game: Mi-do is Dae-su's daughter. He knows, because he arranged it. The fifteen years were never punishment for Dae-su. They were preparation: he was going to relive the sin he was imprisoned for by accident, deliberately, and know it.

Mi-do walks into the room with the gift box that holds the truth. Dae-su begs. He cuts out his own tongue with scissors to stop himself from telling her, and Lee Woo-jin lets the game end there: he walks into his own penthouse lift and shoots himself.

5. The ending

Dae-su takes the truth to the man who hypnotised Lee Woo-jin's sister years ago, and asks for a mercy no film had asked for before: erase Mi-do's memory of that room, and then erase his own. He writes one line on a pad: even though I am worse than an animal, do I not deserve to live?

The film gives us the box. Hypnotism, the hypnotist warns him, never truly deletes anything — everything lives in one's body. The last shot is Mi-do embracing Dae-su in the snow. Her face is calm. His compresses into something like a scream, then relaxes. The film does not tell you whether he remembers. That is the point.

Why it matters

  • It is a revenge film where revenge is the cover story. The real subject is guilt, and how a man's accidental cruelty becomes another man's ruin.
  • Form follows pain. Park Chan-wook's rhythm — hammer, corridor, opera, blood, snowfall — made a whole style of cinema possible.
  • The ending is genuinely double. Everything after the twist can be read as real, as the hypnotist's lie, or as a mercy you accept because the alternative is unbearable.

Questions everyone asks after watching

Does Dae-su remember at the end? Unknowable. The film gives evidence for both readings and refuses the verdict.

Why does he cut his tongue out? To make it physically impossible to tell Mi-do the truth. It is also the film's verdict on his original crime: gossip. A loose tongue cost two families everything.

The octopus? Real, raw, and eaten in one shot by Choi Min-sik — multiple takes. It became the film's internet signature.
