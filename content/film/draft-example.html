Every week someone on Facebook announces that a Sri Lankan cinema is finished. Every week somebody else opens a film to a full hall. Cinema in this country has been "dying" since before most of the people reading this were born, and it is still here — which is why August 20, 2026, is a date worth putting in your calendar.

Photo: SingVaza

The short version (no spoilers)

A drama from director Rajitha Dissanayake opens nationwide on August 20 with a cast of serious names, a story set in a Colombo seen through a rainy window, and a distributor that has given it the kind of rollout a Sinhala film rarely gets. If you have been waiting for a reason to pick Sinhala cinema over a subtitled import this weekend, this is the one I would hand you.

What I will say — and this is not a spoiler, it is the premise — is that the film is interested in the distance between the life people show on a phone screen and the one they go home to. It watches that gap widen for two hours and never once tells you what to think about it.

Who should watch it

  • ✅ You like Sinhala drama that trusts the audience.

Where to watch it (legal places only)

  • Cinema: Scope Cinemas, PVR, Savoy and the main regional halls — check the showtimes before you travel, weekend evening shows are filling up.
  • At home: a Sinhala release usually reaches a streaming platform two to four months after the halls. I will put the name of the platform on this page when it is official — Netflix, Prime Video, Dialog ViU or Peo TV.

Final word

Sinhala cinema does not survive on people saying nice things about it on Facebook. It survives on ticket stubs. This is one worth a stub.
