Two years ago Takashi Yamazaki made a Godzilla film on a budget smaller than most Hollywood catering bills and walked away with the visual-effects Oscar. This week he does it again — and this time the enemy is not just the monster.

The Japanese poster for Godzilla Minus Zero. Photo: Toho

What it is

The direct follow-up to Godzilla Minus One (2023), written and directed again by Takashi Yamazaki, with Ryunosuke Kamiki and Minami Hamabe returning. It is set in 1949 — four years after the last film — and it opens in Japan on 3 November, with North American IMAX early-access shows the same day and the wide US release on 6 November. It is rated R, it is the first Japanese film shot "Filmed for IMAX", and it is sitting at a Tomatometer of around 96% at the time of writing.

Why the first one mattered

Because it was a monster movie about shame. Minus One put a failed kamikaze pilot at the centre and asked what a country does with the people it told to die. It cost a fraction of a Marvel film and looked better than most of them. If you have not seen it, watch it before this one — not because the plot requires it, but because the emotional payoff in the sequel is built on it.

What is confirmed

  • Director: Takashi Yamazaki, returning.
  • Lead cast: Ryunosuke Kamiki, Minami Hamabe.
  • Setting: 1949.
  • Dates: Japan 3 November, US 6 November 2026, IMAX early access from 3 November in North America.

What is only talk

  • How it connects to the 1954 original. Lots of theories online; the filmmakers have not spelled it out.
  • A third film. Everyone has already decided there will be one. Nobody has announced it.

Will it come to Sri Lanka?

Japanese Godzilla films have had limited Sri Lankan releases — usually a short run at one or two Colombo screens, sometimes weeks late. Nothing is confirmed yet. I will put the halls and dates here the moment a distributor lists it, and if it does not get a local release, I will say so plainly rather than pretending a streaming date exists.

Verdict before the fact

This is the release I would put money on this month. Not because a monster film is safe — because the last one was made by people who cared, and there is no reason to think that has changed.
