Every year one film quietly proves that critics do not decide what a country watches. This year it is Verity: 31% from critics, $163 million from audiences, and a screenshot of Anne Hathaway's face that has been in my feed every single day since October.

Three faces, one very bad house. Photo: Amazon MGM Studios

The short version (no spoilers)

A struggling writer is hired to finish a famous author's book series, moves into the family's remote estate, and finds a manuscript she was never meant to read. It is an R-rated psychological thriller, it is much stranger than the trailer, and the reason to watch it is one performance: Anne Hathaway is having the time of her life and she knows exactly what film she is in.

The story, without giving anything away

Lowen Ashleigh (Dakota Johnson) is a writer with no money and a career going nowhere. Jeremy Crawford (Josh Hartnett) offers her the job of a lifetime: finish the remaining books in his wife's bestselling series, because Verity Crawford (Anne Hathaway) is at home, alive, and unable to write another word.

What Lowen finds in that house is an incomplete manuscript, and it is not fiction. That is as far as I go. The film is built on a series of revelations and I am not going to be the one who spoils yours.

What works

  • Anne Hathaway. She plays Verity like a woman who has already won and is enjoying the last few moves. The smile, the stillness, the way she says nothing for eight seconds and makes it the loudest line in the scene. Critics gave this film 31% and still refused to blame her.
  • The house. Remote estate, dark wood, a lake at the bottom of the garden. It does the work that the script sometimes forgets to do.
  • It is fun in a way films are embarrassed to be now. Trashy, camp, a 90s erotic thriller wearing 2026 clothes. You will gasp, then laugh at yourself for gasping.

What does not work

  • It plays it safe. Everything in this film is about obsession, sex and a woman's mind — and the film keeps stepping back from all three. The material wants to be nasty. The film wants to stay polite.
  • The reveal arrives late and lands soft. One twist too late is the phrase critics kept using, and they are right.
  • Dakota Johnson's character is written thinner than her performance. She deserves a film that is as dangerous as she is willing to be.

Who should watch it

  • ✅ You read the book. You were always going to watch it. You will have opinions about the changes.
  • ✅ You like thrillers you can shout at.
  • ✅ You want to see an Oscar winner having an enormous amount of fun.
  • ❌ You want something genuinely dark and unsettling — that film is called Other Mommy, and I reviewed it too.

  • At home: it is already available to buy or rent on Prime Video in several countries, and it has been the most-watched title on that store — so a Prime subscription streaming date is likely to follow. That date is not official yet; when it is, it goes on this page.

Final word

Verity is the best of the Colleen Hoover adaptations and it is still a mess. Watch it for Hathaway, watch it with friends, and do not expect the film the trailer sold you. 6.5/10 — the kind of bad film you will argue about happily afterwards.
