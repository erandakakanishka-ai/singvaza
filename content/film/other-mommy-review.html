This is the film that made me want to start writing about films again. Not because it is good — it is not — but because of what it does to a full cinema at 10.30 at night: thirty-five reviews said it was worthless, nobody in that hall cared, and one scene had grown adults pulling their jackets over their faces. Other Mommy is a bad film that works. That is a much rarer thing than a good one.

Jessica Chastain, smiling the way you will remember her smiling. Photo: Universal Pictures

The short version (no spoilers)

Jessica Chastain plays a mother. She also plays the thing that crawls out of the closet wearing that mother's face, and it is rated R for a reason. The critics were savagely cruel to it and the audience scored it far higher, and both of them are right. If you go in expecting a good script you will be angry. Go in expecting to be uneasy in a way you cannot explain to the person sitting next to you, and you will get your money's worth.

What actually happens (no spoilers — I promise)

A little girl says there is someone in the house. Her mother says there is not. Her mother is wrong, and the thing in the house is wearing her mother's face, and only the child can see the difference.

The film lives in the gap between the mother's smile and what it means. Photo: Universal Pictures

What works

  • Jessica Chastain's body. She does something with her stillness — the head tilting a half-second too late, the smile arriving before the eyes, the way she crosses a room. It is not scary because of the makeup. It is scary because it is almost right. This is the performance people will clip for years.
  • The single best jump scare of the year. You may have seen it in the trailer and it still lands, which should be impossible. The whole hall went up.
  • It does not explain. No rules, no ritual, no wiki-friendly monster logic. That is laziness to some viewers and a deliberate nightmare to others.

What does not work

  • The script. The family's problems are on the screen in capital letters within ten minutes, and the film treats that as a plot. The mother keeps smiling. The father keeps asking if she is okay. That is the movie, for ninety minutes.
  • The ending is chaos. By the last twenty minutes the film stops making sense on purpose and hopes you will not notice. Some of the audience laughed. Some of them were laughing because the alternative was being scared.
  • It is a 93-minute film with maybe 40 minutes of idea. A concept this thin needed to be nastier and shorter, or smarter and longer.

Who should watch it

  • ✅ You want to be uncomfortable in a dark room with strangers and you have a strong stomach.
  • ✅ You love watching a great actor do something genuinely unhinged.
  • ✅ You keep up with horror so you can have an opinion about it.
  • ❌ Not with children, obviously. Not on a date, probably. Not if you dislike films that cheat.

Where to watch it (legal places only)

  • Cinema: in theatres now — check Scope Cinemas and PVR for evening shows, it plays better full and late.
  • At home: not streaming yet. Universal horror titles usually reach digital rental first and a streaming platform after that. I will update this page when it is official — Netflix, Prime Video, Dialog ViU or Peo TV.
  • Please: every "full movie" Telegram channel under a trailer's comments is a stolen copy. The film is still in cinemas — pay for the ticket and the scare.

Final word

Seventeen percent on Rotten Tomatoes, number one at the box office, and a cinema full of people hiding under their coats. Other Mommy is proof that reviews and audiences are measuring different things. I am giving it 5/10 with my head and recommending it with my nervous system.
