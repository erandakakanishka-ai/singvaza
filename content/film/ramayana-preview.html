Four lakh tickets in one day, before a single review has been printed. Whatever you think of ₹1,000-crore epics, that number means something: Ramayana: Rise of a Legend is not opening in cinemas on 5 November, it is arriving as an event. The question everyone is asking is whether it can survive being that big.

Ranbir Kapoor as Rama in the first part of Nitesh Tiwari's two-film adaptation. Photo: Namit Malhotra / Prime Focus

What it is

Part one of a planned two-part telling of the Ramayana, directed by Nitesh Tiwari (Dangal, Chhichhore) and produced by Namit Malhotra. Ranbir Kapoor plays Rama, Sai Pallavi plays Sita, Yash plays Ravana, and Sunny Deol is in the mix as Hanuman. It opens worldwide on Thursday 5 November 2026 — three days before Diwali — in IMAX and 3D, with Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada and Malayalam versions.

Why it matters

India has made the Ramayana on screen many times. It has never made it at this scale: an international IMAX rollout, a cast of that weight, and a budget that has been reported (not confirmed by the studio) in the ₹4,000-crore range across both parts. Advance booking opened on 20 October in a handful of Indian cinemas, and by the end of that month the film had sold roughly 4 lakh tickets in India and earned about $3 million overseas in day-one sales.

What we actually know (confirmed)

  • Release date: 5 November 2026, worldwide, IMAX and 3D.
  • Part two: planned for Diwali 2027.
  • Cast: Ranbir Kapoor, Sai Pallavi, Yash, Sunny Deol.
  • Format versions: Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam.

What is only talk (rumour)

  • Runtime. Numbers between two and a half and three hours are circulating. Unconfirmed — treat any "exact runtime" post as guesswork.
  • Ticket prices. Reports of special pricing in Indian multiplexes have not been confirmed by exhibitors.
  • Cameos. A long list of names is being passed around social media. None of it has come from the producers, so none of it goes in this article as fact.

Will you be able to see it in Sri Lanka?

This is the question I get in my messages every single time. Sri Lankan multiplexes have carried the big Indian releases day-and-date all year, and a film of this size is very likely to open here on or near 5 November — but do not book travel on my word. Check Scope Cinemas, PVR Colombo and Savoy for the listing before you go; they publish showtimes a few days ahead. If and when the Sri Lankan shows are confirmed, this page gets updated with the actual dates and halls.

Verdict before the fact

Twelve years ago a Ramayana television serial stopped the country at 8.30 every night. A cinema release cannot do that, but it can do something the serial could not: give the story a screen big enough for the scale it has always deserved. I will be there on the first weekend, and the review will be here — in this same style, no spoilers, straight about what works and what does not.
